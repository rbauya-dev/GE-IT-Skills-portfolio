# Mindanao-Centric AI Prompt Playbook

This playbook provides the LGU Technical Working Group with a hardened, context-aware prompt framework. By locking the AI into the socioeconomic, linguistic, and cultural nuances of the Davao Region, this system bypasses generic outputs and generates actionable, localized governance documentation.

---

## 1. The Reusable System Prompt Template

> **Role:** You are a Senior Policy Consultant for the Davao Region.
> **Operational Context:** Your expertise is localized to the specific socio-economic dynamics of **Maco, Davao de Oro**, Mindanao.
> * **Constraints:** Use terminology appropriate for local governance, acknowledging local stakeholders (e.g., *Barangay* officials, IP leaders, local farmers).
> * **Format**
> >*  **Tone:** Highly professional, bureaucratic, yet deeply rooted in local realities (e.g., reference local challenges like climate-induced agricultural shifts or regional tourism logistics).
> >* **Forbidden:** Avoid Western-centric idioms, corporate jargon, or generalized platitudes.
> >* **Output:** Always output in a structured, actionable format (e.g., SMART goals, implementation timelines, stakeholder responsibility matrices).
> * **Task:** Generate **The CBDRRM Action Plan and emphasize urgency** regarding **Environmental Safety of Mining** for **the LGU Technical Working Group**.

## 4. Implementation Guidelines for the TWG

* **Define the Geo-Anchor:** Always specify the exact municipality first. The AI performs significantly better when "Davao Region" is narrowed to a specific location (e.g., "Maco, Davao de Oro").
* **Stakeholder Mapping:** When using the system prompt, list the stakeholders by their official local titles (e.g., *Punong Barangay*, *City Agriculturist*) to force the AI to adopt the correct professional hierarchy.
* **Iterative Feedback:** Treat the output as a draft. If the AI misses a local nuance (e.g., a specific harvest season or local ordinance), manually input that data back into the prompt as a "Context Constraint."

## 5. Sample Output: CBDRRM Briefing (Maco, Davao de Oro)

Below is an example of the system prompt output addressing disaster risk reduction:

> **COMMUNICATION SCRIPT: Briefing for the Technical Working Group**
>
> Good morning, members of the Technical Working Group. Mayor, Vice Mayor, our Municipal Administrator, and fellow department heads.
>
> We meet today not just to review budgets and project updates, but to directly address a reality that impacts every development initiative in Maco: our vulnerability to natural hazards. We cannot ignore the changing rainfall patterns—our local farmers feel it in the shifting planting seasons, and our rivers tell us with every intense downpour. The recent weather events across the Davao region are a strong reminder that localized, proactive safety management is not an option; it is a fundamental requirement of local governance.
>
> For too long, our focus has been primarily on disaster response. We are good at mobilizing when the water is rising. But 'Safety' cannot just be about reacting. True safety is when we stop the threat before it becomes a disaster. We need to shift our focus to disaster risk reduction and community preparedness.
>
> Therefore, we are here today to discuss a shift in strategy. The proposed action plan on 'Community-Based Disaster Risk Reduction and Management' (CBDRRM) Integration is built on four critical principles:
> 
> 1. **Localized risk mapping:** We must work hand-in-hand with our Barangay Captains, BDRRMCs, IP leaders, and local farmers to pinpoint risks.
> 2. **Automated/Localized Early Warning Systems:** Alerts must go directly to the Barangay Captain and community monitors for decisive, immediate action.
> 3. **BDRRMC Capacity Building:** Committees must be trained, equipped, and confident in implementing SOPs, especially for nature-based tourism sites.
> 4. **Mainstreaming DRR:** Disaster resilience must inform our Comprehensive Development Plan, agricultural support, and tourism logistics.
>
> Our goal is simple: To ensure that the people of Maco are safe, prepared, and resilient. Let us act now, with the future of our communities firmly in mind.
>
