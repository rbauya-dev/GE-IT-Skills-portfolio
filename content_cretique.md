## 1. Literature Review Summary: Economic Performance of the Davao Region (2025)

The macroeconomic trajectory of the Davao Region in 2025 was characterized by significant output expansion counterbalanced by severe environmental and infrastructural vulnerabilities. According to the Philippine Statistics Authority (PSA) Davao Region, the region's Gross Regional Domestic Product (GRDP) surpassed the trillion-peso mark, reaching ₱1.14 trillion at constant 2018 prices. This represents a 5.1% annual growth rate. While this marks a deceleration from the 6.3% growth recorded in 2024, the region outperformed the national GDP average of 4.4% and ranked as the fourth fastest-growing regional economy in the country, maintaining its position as Mindanao's largest economy.

Growth was categorically uneven across sectors. The services sector remained the foundational driver of the region, accounting for 62.1% of the total economic output (₱707.15 billion) and expanding by 6.9%. The industry sector comprised 24.7% of the economy, growing by 2.7%, heavily buoyed by a 13.4% surge in public administration, defense, and compulsory social security. Notably, agriculture, forestry, and fishing (13.2% of GRDP) demonstrated a vital rebound, growing by 1.9% after contracting by 0.4% in 2024.

However, local development literature and economic briefings explicitly contextualize this top-line growth against compounding grassroots challenges. The region faced severe disruptions from twin earthquakes and recurring extreme weather disturbances throughout 2025. These events damaged agricultural yields, strained local transport networks, and brought public scrutiny upon the efficacy and transparency of regional flood mitigation and infrastructure projects. Consequently, while formal macroeconomic indicators remain robust, localized resilience—particularly regarding communal infrastructure, waste management, and environmental adaptation—emerges as a critical, under-addressed pressure point in the region's development pipeline.

## 3. Structural Bias & Western-Centric Framing Audit

The synthesis of this literature reveals a persistent Western-centric macroeconomic bias. The reliance on Gross Regional Domestic Product (GRDP) as the ultimate barometer of regional success adheres strictly to neoliberal frameworks that quantify formal, market-based transactions.

This framing structurally marginalizes the informal economy, which sustains a vast portion of Davao's population. By categorizing economic health primarily through formal industry and services, the reports fail to capture the economic weight of decentralized, community-led survival mechanisms. Grassroots initiatives—such as communal rainwater harvesting networks, indigenous agricultural practices, or informal waste management—are rendered invisible. Consequently, policy papers derived purely from this literature risk optimizing for formal state spending while neglecting the hyper-local interventions that shield vulnerable *barangays* from systemic shocks.
